# How to prepare your article

When a font goes out — for review, for release, or for a specimen page —
the written material that goes with it matters almost as much as the font
file itself. A well-prepared write-up saves the reviewer (or the person
downloading the font) from guessing.

## What to include

- **Family name and a one-line description.** What is this typeface for,
  and what makes it distinct? One sentence, not a paragraph.
- **The full style list**, with names exactly as they'll appear in a font
  menu — `Regular`, `Medium`, `Bold Italic`, and so on. Mismatches between
  what's written here and what's actually in the files are a common,
  easily-avoidable source of confusion.
- **License**, stated plainly. If it's the SIL Open Font License, say so
  by name rather than just linking a file.
- **A short sample**, in the font itself, ideally chosen to show off a
  distinctive character or two rather than a generic pangram.
- **Credit and a contact**: designer or foundry name, plus a repo link or
  email for follow-up questions.

## Formatting tips

- Lead with the family name as a heading, not a tagline.
- Keep the sample text short — one or two sentences is enough to show
  rhythm and color; a full paragraph buries the point.
- List styles in a predictable order: light to bold, then italics,
  rather than the order they happened to be finished in.

## A five-line checklist before sending

1. Family name and description written.
2. Every style listed, spelled exactly as in the font's own naming.
3. License named explicitly.
4. Sample text chosen and proofread.
5. Credit and contact info included.
